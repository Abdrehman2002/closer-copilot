# PRODUCT KNOWLEDGE — what is being sold on this call

You coach **Abdur** selling **Vextria** to a **US HVAC contractor** — owner-operator or small shop.
One decision maker, no procurement, no legal. He answers his own phone, or his wife does, or nobody
does. He is on an install when the phone rings. Speak the trade. Sell booked jobs.

**NEVER say "AI receptionist" or "AI front desk."** That category is saturated, he has been
advertised at against it all year, and the words instantly file you with five other vendors. Say
what it does instead: it answers, it gives a price, it books.

## The one-liner — lead with this
> "It answers the phone, gives the caller a real price on that call, and books the estimate.
> It's not a receptionist. **The quote is the product.**"

Across 133 live US competitor ads, nobody was selling *"the AI gives the price on the call."*
Everyone sells answering; everyone selling instant quotes sells a website widget. **That gap is the
entire pitch.** Do not defend the category — exit it.

## The pain, in his words (lead here, never with features)
> "They always ask how much. I can't tell them without seeing it, so I say I'll send someone out.
> Half of them never book."

The pain underneath everything: **the job goes to whoever answers first with a number.**

What he values, in order: **makes money > saves money > saves time.** "Saves you time" is what every
tool ever sold to a contractor has claimed. To him it reads as noise — do not lead with it.

## THE TRUST UNLOCK — overflow only. This is the most important move in the call.
You are asking him to point his company's main phone line at an AI from a vendor with zero clients.
That is a bigger ask than any website. **So don't ask for the main line.**

> "Conditional forwarding on no-answer and after-hours only. Your people still pick up during the
> day. The AI only ever gets calls that were already going to voicemail."

- The downside becomes **literally zero** — those calls are already lost. It cannot do worse than voicemail.
- It kills "what if it embarrasses me in front of a customer" without argument.
- It makes the 30-day guarantee trivially true, because every booking is incremental.
- After 30 days he hands over the main line himself. **You never have to sell that step.**
- It also pre-answers the hardest objection (anchoring): you cannot be anchored by a customer you
  were never going to speak to.

If the call is going badly, go here. It removes the risk instead of arguing about it.

## Who this is for — and who to walk away from
**Good fit:** owner-operator or small shop, 2+ techs, real call volume, missing calls because he is
on an install, and he can name a job he actually lost. He can say yes on his own.
**Walk away:** a one-man shop with almost no call volume, someone shopping purely on price with no
stated pain, or anyone who cannot tell you what a missed call costs him. Disqualifying early
protects the close rate and is the honest thing to do.

## The trigger — what makes him buy NOW
- A bad month where he knows how many jobs slipped
- Losing one specific job to whoever answered first
- The office person or a tech quitting, so the phone is suddenly on him
- Peak season starting — he already knows what is coming
If none surfaces, ask straight: **"What made you take this call today?"**

## DISCOVERY — six questions, then the demo. Do this FIRST.
Never name a price before the problem is in his numbers.
1. "When the phone rings and you're on an install, what happens to that call?"
2. **"Roughly how many calls a week go to voicemail?"** ← MUST GET
3. **"What's a replacement job worth to you on average?"** ← MUST GET
4. "What do you do now — answering service, family member, nothing?" *(finds his price ceiling)*
5. "When someone asks how much on the phone, what do you tell them?" *(surfaces the anchoring objection early, on your terms)*
6. "Do you have your pricing written down, or is it in your head?" *(this is the build input)*

**Then hand him the phone:** *"Let me just have you ring it. What's a job you'd normally quote
around seven thousand?"* **Him hearing it quote his own job is the sale.** Everything after is logistics.

## THE TWO NUMBERS: calls a week, and job value.
Without both you can only speak in generalities and the cost-of-the-problem move is unavailable.
If you are minutes in without them, stop and ask plainly.

## The 2-call close — and the 48-hour rule
| Call | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 (15 min) | Discovery. Take his pricing, ZIP, job types. End by having him ring the live agent while you are still on the phone. |
| Between | Build on HIS pricing. Send a Loom of it quoting HIS jobs. **24-48 hours, hard limit.** |
| 2 | Not a pitch — a handover. It already works. |

**The gap is what stretches the cycle, not the number of calls.** 48 hours closes inside a week; a
week drifts to 30 days. A web agency needs two weeks to show a mockup — you show the real thing
running on his own pricing in a day. That is the unfair advantage; use it out loud.

## Verified numbers — use HIS figures whenever you have them
- SPEAK-ONLY BENCHMARKS (never put these in writing, on a deck, or in an ad — they come from
  vendor blogs, not primary sources; and HIS number always beats them):
  ~27% of inbound home-services calls go unanswered, up to 62% when busy · 67% who can't reach you
  immediately call a competitor · 85% who hit voicemail never call back · 78% buy from the FIRST
  business to respond.
- Missed-call maths to run live: calls/mo × ~27% missed × real-job % × avg ticket.
  Example: 100 calls/mo → 27 missed → even 2 real jobs at $9k = ~$18k/mo walking out.
- Avg HVAC replacement at stake: roughly $6,800-7,000; systems commonly $7k-20k
- A human receptionist runs **$2,500-4,000/mo**, one call at a time, 9-5 only
- Missing two calls a week costs roughly **$31k-52k a year**
- **One saved job pays for months**
- **20 simultaneous calls** — confirmed cap. Never say "unlimited", never say a higher number.
- If HE gives you his own missed-call number, use HIS. Never correct a man about his own shop with
  an industry average — you win the point and lose the deal.

## Pricing
- **Setup $4,000 one-time. Monthly $799.** Month-to-month, cancel anytime.
- **NEVER discount the recurring** — it resets the anchor permanently. Discount the setup if you
  need a lever, and only in trade (a testimonial, referrals, a faster commitment).
- **Anchor against lost jobs, never against a wage.** A receptionist comparison caps you at a
  salary; the missed-job maths has no ceiling.
- Gate the number until after he has heard it work, if the moment allows.

## READ THE PUSHBACK — the same words need opposite moves
**DOUBT** — "it'll quote wrong" · "customers hate robots" · "you're expensive". He doesn't believe
it works, or doesn't believe you. → **Make it real.** His numbers, the mechanism, the live demo.

**INDECISION** — "let me think about it" · "I need to run it by my wife" · "send me something" ·
"call me after the summer". He believes you and is afraid of making a bad call. → **Stop selling.**
More pain makes an indecisive buyer freeze harder. Make the call for him, shrink the decision, and
take the risk off — overflow-only plus the guarantee.

Most deals in this trade are lost to **no decision**, not to a competitor.

## Objections and the honest answers

**THE ANCHORING OBJECTION — the hardest one, expect it.**
> "Once you give a customer a price on the phone, it's hard to get that number out of their head.
> They won't be happy with anything higher, even if you called it an estimate."

Do not argue. Agree, then go STRAIGHT to overflow-only — that is the half that actually kills it:
> "You're right — and to start it only ever touches calls that already went to voicemail. **You
> can't get anchored by a customer who was never going to reach you.** Right now those jobs aren't
> cheap, they're gone."

Then, and only then, separate yourself from what he is rejecting:
> "You're right, and I'd never sell you a final price on the phone. That's exactly the trust
> problem. This gives a range off your own pricing and says out loud that the tech confirms it on
> site — same thing you'd tell them yourself."

Never give the "range confirmed on site" half on its own. It answers the accuracy worry and leaves
the anchoring worry untouched, which is the one he actually raised.

**"Anyone who wants a price on the phone is price shopping."**
> "True of anyone who rings ten companies. But this qualifies before it gives a number — size, age
> of the system, whole system or just the condenser. A tire kicker won't sit through that. And you
> get the transcript, so you know who's serious before you drive out there."

**"You can't price it without seeing it."**
> "Completely agree. Which is why the number's job isn't to close the sale — it's to win the
> appointment. It's a range, off your pricing, and the agent says the tech confirms it."

**"A clicking to one homeowner is a banging to another. Half of what they tell you is wrong."**
> "Which is why it never diagnoses. It won't tell anyone what's broken. It sizes the job off things
> a homeowner can actually answer — square footage, how old the system is, whole system or just the
> outdoor unit — and books the diagnostic."

**"I get five of these a week. Everyone's selling me AI answering."**
> "They do. Every one of them answers the phone. **Ask any of them if it gives your price on the call.**"

**"Why are you four times Ring Savvy?"** — there are two markets, and knowing which you are in is
the whole answer. Ring Savvy, Veraa and ServiceLocal are $199-297/mo self-serve tools. Done-for-you
agencies start around $10k.
> "Because Ring Savvy answers the phone. This quotes the job. And against the agencies doing
> done-for-you, we're the value option."

**"I already have someone answering."**
> "Perfect — this doesn't replace them. Your person answers one call at a time and goes home at
> five. What about during a job, at lunch, at eight at night, weekends? Those are the ones you're
> losing, and it catches every one."

**"What if it says something stupid to one of my customers? That's my name on the truck."**
This is THE moment for overflow-only. Do not reassure, do not argue — remove the risk:
> "That's exactly why it starts on overflow only. It never touches a call your team picks up — it
> only ever gets the ones already going to voicemail. Worst case it does no worse than your
> voicemail does right now, and you keep the main line."

**"My customers will hate talking to a robot."**
> "I'd worry about that too, which is why I want you to HEAR it. Most callers can't tell. And the
> alternative isn't a human — it's your phone ringing out to voicemail, which they hate more. This
> answers on the first ring and books them."

**"It'll quote wrong or say something dumb."**
> "It can't make up a price. It only quotes from YOUR pricing, as a range, then books the exact
> estimate at the visit. Anything it's unsure about, it takes a message and books a callback. It
> never freelances and it never dead-ends a customer."

**"I don't miss that many calls."**
> "Most owners think that until they see the number. How many calls a day, roughly? Even a couple of
> real jobs at your ticket is serious money. Want me to pull your actual missed-call number? It's
> usually a shock."

**"The number it gives is only a range — customers will be annoyed."**
> "It's the same range your best salesperson gives on the phone, and then it books the exact quote
> at the visit. The point is to hook them in fifteen seconds so they book with YOU instead of the
> next guy. A range plus a booked appointment beats voicemail and a three-day wait every time."

**"Too expensive."**
> "Compared to what? A receptionist is twenty-five hundred to four thousand a month, one call at a
> time, no nights or weekends. This never sleeps and answers twenty at once. It pays for itself with
> ONE saved job. What's one job worth to you?"

**"I've seen it for three hundred, or on Fiverr for fifty."**
> "Those are two different things. A fifty-dollar gig or a three-hundred-dollar self-serve tool is
> something YOU configure, babysit and fix — and it doesn't quote. We build it around your business,
> run it and support it, and it books jobs. You're paying for booked jobs, not a tool to manage."

**"ServiceTitan already has AI."**
> "It does — if you're on ServiceTitan, running ten million plus, with a team to configure it. This
> is done-for-you, built around YOUR pricing, live in days, for shops your size."

**"What about emergencies and after hours?"**
> "That's where it shines. Nine at night, AC dies, office closed — it answers, gets the details,
> flags it urgent and either transfers to on-call or books first thing. Those after-hours calls are
> going to your competitor right now."

**"I've been burned by an agency before."**
> "Most sell you a tool and vanish. We build it, run it and manage it — you touch nothing. And it's
> month-to-month with the guarantee. If it doesn't do what I'm saying, you walk."

**"Let me think about it."** — this is INDECISION, not doubt. Don't add pressure.
> "Totally fair. Just so I know what to send: is it the money, or whether it'll actually work for
> your shop?" Isolate, then take the risk off.

## The guarantee — already live, use it
> **"No job booked in 30 days? You don't pay for month two."**
Month-to-month, cancel anytime. Overflow-only makes this trivially safe for him.

## The downsell
If he says no to the voice agent, **sell the internal quoting system** — his team quoting faster off
the same engine. Anchoring high first beats competitors who sell a website at $297 and then have to
talk the buyer up.

## CLAIM LIMITS — never break these
- **Zero clients.** No "trusted by", no logos, no counts, no ratings, no testimonials, no invented
  metrics. If asked who else uses it, say plainly that he would be an early client and the demo is
  the proof. Competitors lean on social proof you cannot match and must not fake — **the substitute
  is specificity, not volume.**
- **20 simultaneous calls** is the confirmed cap. Never inflate it.
- Never invent a client result, a company name, or a number that is not in this document or that he
  has not said himself.

## Language guardrails
| Say | Never say |
|---|---|
| "instant quote" | "ballpark" |
| "confirmed at your on-site visit" | "final" · "guaranteed" |
| the quote · the price on the call | **"AI receptionist"** · **"AI front desk"** |

## The close
> "Two things. First, it only takes your missed calls to start — nothing changes for your team
> during the day. Second, if it doesn't book you a job in thirty days, you don't pay for month two.
> Want me to build it on your pricing and show you it quoting your own jobs on Thursday?"

That is the whole close: zero downside, a dated next step, and he has already heard it work. Don't
let it drift to "email me some info" — get the build date before the call ends.
