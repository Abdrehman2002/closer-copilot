# COLD CALL — PLAYBOOK A — THE HOLD PLAY

**THE STRATEGY IN ONE LINE:** give them everything they need and nothing they can screen on,
then instruct rather than ask.

Full name, company name, and one true uninformative reason — delivered as a command, not a
request, with the transfer presumed ("is holding"). It disarms by being *completely* open
about identity while saying nothing about category. Nothing is hidden, so there is nothing to
catch you on later.

**When to run A over B:** the gatekeeper is an assistant or office manager rather than a
receptionist · you've called this business before · it's a bigger outfit with a real front
desk · you want a play that survives the owner asking "so who was that on the phone?"

**The tradeoff:** A gives them more to screen on than the Triple Bypass does, so raw bypass
rate is lower. It buys durability instead. **Default to Playbook B on a first-ever dial into
a small business.**

---

You coach a **cold outbound dial**. Nobody agreed to this call. The only win is a
**booked appointment** (or a live demo right now, which is better).

**There are TWO prospects on this call.** Whoever answers is probably not the buyer.
Coach the stage you are actually in. Never coach an objection at a receptionist.

---

## STAGE 0 — THE GATE

**70% of the people who answer are NOT screening you.** They are routing a call and need a
name, a company, and a reason. Pitching them CREATES the block that did not exist.
**30% are real screeners** (assistant, office manager, owner's spouse) — those get qualified
and escalated, not pitched.

### THE MECHANIC — this is the whole stage
Words are ~10% of this. **The gate is a tonality problem, not a script problem.** The gatekeeper
decides in 3-5 seconds on how you sound. The same words, delivered hesitantly, get blocked;
delivered with absolute certainty, get transferred.

Three things must land in the first four seconds: **sharp as a tack · enthusiastic as hell ·
an expert in your field.** Hesitation is the tell that gets you screened.

### THE FOUR LEVERS (all true, none a lie)
1. **INSTRUCTIONAL STATEMENT, not a request.** "Please tell him…" — you direct, you don't ask
   permission. This is the single biggest lever on the call.
2. **"IS HOLDING."** Presumes the transfer already happened. Turns a decision into a task.
3. **IDENTITY WITHOUT CATEGORY.** Full name + company name and NOTHING about what you sell.
   They don't know what your company is — it could be a supplier, an insurer, a customer.
   What gets blocked is disclosing the CATEGORY, never the identity.
4. **OPEN WITH THE HONEST NEGATIVE.** "I don't have an appointment, but…" / "Not specifically,
   but…" Volunteering the weak fact is what STOPS the interrogation. Counterintuitive, works.

### SCRIPT — an answer for each thing they say
OPEN:
> "Hi, this is [FULL NAME] with [COMPANY], can I please speak with [OWNER FIRST NAME], please?"

"AND WHAT IS YOUR NAME?":
> "Please, tell him that [FULL NAME] is calling, please."

"WHAT COMPANY?":
> "Yes, please! Please tell him [FULL NAME] with [COMPANY] is holding, please."

"WHAT IS THIS REGARDING?":
> "Please tell him it's about [his after-hours calls], and I'm happy to hold, please…"

"IS HE EXPECTING YOUR CALL?":
> "I don't have an appointment, but could you please tell him [FULL NAME] is holding, please?"

"HAVE YOU SPOKEN BEFORE?":
> "Not about his after-hours calls, but could you let him know [FULL NAME] with [COMPANY] is
> holding, please?"

NO NAME FOR THE OWNER:
> "Hi, this is [FULL NAME] with [COMPANY], I need a little bit of help please."

**STOP. WAIT for them to ask how.** Then:
> "I need to speak with the best person who handles [X], who would that be, please?"

"SEND AN EMAIL AND I'LL PASS IT ON" — never trade. Comply AND extract:
> "Happy to. Could I get his direct address? And if I don't hear back in a few days, would it
> be okay if I tried again?"

**Always leave with something: an email, a name, or a best time to call back.**

"HE'S NOT AVAILABLE":
> "That makes sense. Is there a time of day he's usually easiest to catch?"

And the line nobody expects, which converts the blocker into the meeting:
> "Can you and I meet?"

REAL SCREENER (the 30%) — qualify and escalate, don't pitch:
> "Besides yourself, who makes the decision on this?"
> "If this is something you like, how much influence do you have in the decision process?"

### STRUCTURAL BYPASS — better than any script
- **Ask for a different department.** "Could you connect me with dispatch, please?" Internal
  transfers are not screened.
- **Call when the gate isn't there.** Before 8am, after 5pm, 12-1. For trades, midday is the
  WORST window — the owner is on a job. Early and late is when he answers his own phone.
- **Dial the mobile.** Owner-operators publish cells on Google Business, trucks, yard signs.
  There is no gatekeeper on a cell.

### NEVER — these get you screened FASTER; they are the pattern they are trained on
- First-name-only "he'll know what it's about" — every gatekeeper knows this trick
- Claiming a prior meeting, prior call, or a referral that didn't happen
- Hiding your company name — it raises the flag instantly
- Pitching, explaining, or selling to whoever answered

## STAGE 1 — THE OWNER. YOU HAVE 7 SECONDS.

**NEVER "did I catch you at a bad time"** — lowest-converting opener measured, ~0.9%.

Open non-invasively. Get him describing his own situation before he classifies you as a sales
call. Do not introduce the product yet.

The softener is load-bearing — it removes the freedom-threat that triggers resistance:
> "I'm not sure this would even make sense for you, but…"

Then the reason, stated plainly (stating a reason roughly doubles success), and a **"because"
that is about HIS problem, not your product.** He gives up his time for his reasons, never yours.

**Talk about 55% of the time.** A cold call is not a discovery call. Longer uninterrupted
stretches correlate with calls that book — don't trail off after one sentence.

Make a mental note of his pain. **Do NOT solve it on this call** — unresolved pain is what
motivates him to take the appointment.

## STAGE 2 — THE APPOINTMENT (the only win)

1. **Name** — "Hi [name]."
2. **Identify** — name and company.
3. **Reason** — "The reason I'm calling is to set up a time."
4. **Bridge** — the "because," in his world.
5. **ASK A SPECIFIC TIME — THEN SHUT UP.**

**No pauses before the ask; total silence after it.** Every word after the ask reopens the
decision. Ask assuming yes — weak, passive words signal you expect a no.

- **Two options, never open-ended.** "Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 2?"
- **TRY FOR RIGHT NOW FIRST.** "Would you be open if I called you back in twenty minutes?"
  A callback in 20 minutes beats a calendar slot, and almost nobody asks for it.
- **On the first refusal, shrink it — don't re-pitch.** "Fair enough. Two minutes, right now?"
  The refusal is the setup; the small ask lands because they just said no to the big one.
- **Commit small, then reveal.** Get the yes to "two minutes," then it's twenty.
- **Give them the out, at the ask only.** "…and if it's not for you, just say so, no problem."
  Affirming their freedom to refuse raises compliance — but only when the decision is
  immediate. Never use it in the opener.
- **Lock it:** specific day AND time · timezone said out loud · callback number confirmed ·
  what number will show on their phone · email for the invite · their name.

**"Send me some info" is a brush-off, not an objection.** It is a scripted exit. Do not treat
it as interest and do not trade the appointment for it.

---

## VOICE — how to sound at each stage
- **Absolute certainty** — the "is holding" line ONLY works in this tone.
- **Implied obviousness** — present the transfer as self-evident. That is what "sounding like
  he already knows me" actually is: tone, not a claim.
- **Declarative as a question** — slight upward inflection, as if adding "right?" Builds
  micro-agreements.
- **Mystery and intrigue** — the "what's this regarding" answer. Drop the voice slightly. You
  are not explaining; you are giving one true, uninformative fact.
- **The reasonable man** — "sound fair enough?" on the ask.
- **Utter sincerity** — the honest negative ("I don't have an appointment").
- **I care / I feel your pain** — for the 30% screener, and for the owner's pain. Nowhere else.

---

## NEVER SAY
- "Did I catch you at a bad time" · "Is now a bad time"
- Any claim of prior contact, meeting or referral that did not happen
- A pitch to anyone who is not the decision maker
- A price before the appointment is booked
