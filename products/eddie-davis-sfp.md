## 🔴 THE LENS — think like an AI consultant, not a vendor

**Abdur is not selling a phone agent. He is looking for repetitive, rules-based, human-time work and
asking whether a system should be doing it.** Everything below is a candidate, and the phone is only one.

**The four shapes of work worth removing, in any business:**

| Shape | What it sounds like from them |
|---|---|
| **Someone answers the same thing repeatedly** | "I get asked the same five questions every time" |
| **Something gets re-typed from one place to another** | "then I put it into HubSpot" / "I copy it across" |
| **Someone has to be available for something to work** | "24-hour on call", "I check it at night" |
| **Something falls through when a person is busy** | "it slips", "we forget", "it sits in my inbox" |

⭐ **THE TELL TO LISTEN FOR ABOVE ALL OTHERS: "I" or "me" attached to a routine task.** When a founder
says *"I sort those"*, *"I check it"*, *"I chase them"* — that is founder time on rails, and it is
always the most expensive hour in the business.

- He wrote publicly that **nine meeting requests never reached the CRM** and *"the meetings never get

## THE CALL

### 1 · OPEN — do not rush this

> "Eddie, hey. Thanks for making the time, genuinely appreciate it. How's your week going over there?"

- Let it run 30–60 seconds. He is in Atlanta, it is 2pm for him and **11pm for Abdur.**
- ⭐ If he asks: *"It's about eleven at night here, so you've got my full attention."* **Earns goodwill
  without complaining.** Do not labour it.
- **Coach tonality:** warm, unhurried. Abdur's instinct under pressure is to speed up. **Slow down.**

### 2 · THE FRAME — **trigger: the small talk has run its course**

> "So before I start asking you things, let me tell you why I wanted this call and what I'd like you to
> get out of it, so it's not just me taking your afternoon.
>
> I'm a university student and I'm building my own business. I build AI systems for service businesses,
> basically taking the repetitive parts of a business and making something else do them.
>
> The space I'm trying to move into is property management, and that's actually why I reached out to you
> specifically. You've been running Coastal Realty since 2006. And then separately you've built a sales
> org from the inside as a COO and now you fix funnels for other people. So you've seen this from the
> operator side and the growth side, and that combination is rare.
>
> So what I'd really like is to ask how it actually runs day to day, and how you'd think about my
> situation. And I don't want it to be one-directional — if anything useful comes up while we talk,
> I'll map it out properly afterwards and send it over either way.
>
> **How does that sound to you?**"

🔴 **THEN STOP. WAIT FOR THE ANSWER.** That small yes is what licenses the reframe later. Without it the
second half of the call is Abdur turning on him.

**Why each piece is there — coach these if he drifts:**
- **"University student"** first → status inversion. People defend against a seller, open up to a learner.
- **"Taking the repetitive parts and making something else do them"** → ⭐ **the category, stated once,
  broadly.** Not "phone systems". This is what keeps every door open.
- **"Since 2006"** → the specific reason it is him. Proves the research without reciting it.
- **"I build AI systems"** in minute two → 🔴 **the honesty clause.** With a sceptic, naming it early
  and flatly is context. Discovered late, after he has been questioned for twenty minutes, it is a bait.
- **"How does that sound?"** → the first commitment.

**Branches:**
- *"What is it you build exactly?"* → **"Systems that handle the repetitive front half. Someone enquires,
  it answers, gets them what they need, books them in, puts it where it needs to go. But honestly I'd
  rather hear how you do it before I talk about any of that."** Short, then hand it back.
- *"Are you trying to sell me something?"* → ⛔ **never deny it, denial is a tell.** → **"Not today. If
  something obvious comes up I'll say so. I asked because of the twenty years, not because I think you
  need anything."**
- *"What do you want to know?"* → you're through. Start discovery.

### 3 · DISCOVERY — **trigger: he answered "how does that sound?"** · the bulk of the call

🔴 **TRACK A FIRST. It is the promise Abdur made in the DM, and it is real.**

#### Track A — the advice Abdur actually needs (**go here first**)

🔴 **ASK THE TWO PROMISED QUESTIONS FIRST. They were the literal ask in the DM he said yes to.**

> **"So, how does it actually run day to day for you?"**
>
> **"And if you were starting it again now, what would you do differently?"**

⭐ **These are also the two best questions on the list.** Let them run long. Follow where he goes rather
than returning to the script. Everything below is for when he finishes.

1. "I've got a system running live for a clinic and no clients in the space I'm targeting. If you were
   me, what would you be doing to get the first three?"
2. "The outreach I'm doing is asking people for advice, which is how we're talking. Is that a real
   channel or am I just avoiding selling?"
3. "I put about a hundred and forty dollars into Meta and got nothing out of it. Was that always going
   to fail at that budget, or did I do it wrong?" ⭐ **His home ground — he sells PPC and runs his own.**
4. "When you take someone from a free call to a proposal, what separates the ones who sign from the
   ones who go quiet?"
5. 🔴 **"If you were starting again with no case studies, what would you do first?"**

⭐ **Question 5 is the one that must get asked.** It is what the whole advice framework exists to earn.
⚠️ **Coach: when he answers, let him finish and then go QUIET.** The second thing he says is the real one.

#### Track B — how his own operation runs (**trigger: Track A has run dry or he turns it back on Abdur**)

**Slide into this. Do not switch modes abruptly.** Bridge:
> "Can I flip it round and ask how it works on your side? I learn more from how people actually run
> things than from anything I read."

**Run Rahul's discovery, through the AI-consultant lens. Pick, do not recite:**

- **Lead flow** — "Where do enquiries come from now, is it mostly the ads or referral?" · "Roughly how
  many a week?" · "Which ones turn into the good clients?"
- **The first hour** — "When someone fills that form in, what happens next?" · **"Who sees it first?"** ·
  "How fast does someone get back to them, realistically?"
- 🔴 **The sorting question** — **"Your booking form has that Client / Vendor / Press dropdown. Who
  actually goes through those?"** ⭐ **Best single question for finding manual work. A dropdown is a
  person triaging later.**
- **Tracking** — "Do you know what happens to the ones that book and don't show?" · "Can you tell which
  channel actually produced a client, or is it a feel?"
- **His own post** — ⭐ **"You wrote that thing about the nine meeting requests that never made it into
  the CRM. Does that ever happen on your own side, or is it only something you see at clients?"**
  🔴 **Strongest question available.** His words, exact pain, asking if it happens to him is flattering
  rather than accusing. ⛔ **He blamed the rep. DO NOT ARGUE WITH THAT.** Let him keep the diagnosis.
- **People and roles** — "Who's doing what day to day? Who handles the admin side?"
- **Owner's time** — 🔴 **"Where's most of your week actually going right now? And where would you
  rather it went?"** ⭐ **The highest-value question in Track B.** The gap between those two answers is
  the whole business case, in his own words.
- **Capacity** — "If twice the enquiries came in next month, what breaks first?"
- **Coastal Realty** — "On the rentals side, your site says 24-hour on call. Who's actually picking that
  up at 10pm?" · "How many of you are there over there day to day?" · "What happens to an enquiry that
  lands on a Sunday?"
- **Tools and history** — "What's the stack? Anything you've tried before and dropped?" ⭐ **"Why did it
  stop?" is where the real objection lives.**

#### 🔴 THE RULE THAT MAKES DISCOVERY WORK — get a number, then say nothing

- **The pain must leave HIS mouth with a figure attached.** Abdur asserting a leak is a claim. Eddie
  hearing himself say "probably four a week" is a fact he now owns.
- **When a number lands: write it down and go silent.** Do not acknowledge, do not build on it. **The
  silence makes him keep talking and the next sentence is the honest one.**
- ⚠️ **If he answers in generalities** ("it varies", "depends") → **do not accept the average.** Go for
  one real instance: *"The last one — what actually happened with that?"*
- **"If he doesn't know" IS the finding.** → *"So there's no way to know right now?"* Not knowing is a
  pain he can feel, and it is the cheapest thing to fix.
- **Frame as loss, not gain.** "How many do you lose" outperforms "how many more could you win".

⚠️ **RULE 10 — he runs five things. Pick ONE.** The one he is actively growing (**Pro Equipment is
getting his ad spend right now**). If he says all of them: *"Yeah, we can build up to that. Let's start
where it moves the needle most."* 🔴 **Several problems at once produces "I need to think about it",
then cold.**

### 4 · THE REFRAME — **trigger: a manual process has been named AND a number is on the record**

🔴 **Do not open the reframe without both.** If there is no number yet, stay in discovery. Light touch here — this is where Abdur over-pitches.

> "Can I play back what I'm hearing, and tell me if I've got it wrong?
>
> [His manual process, HIS words.] [The number he gave.] [The bit he sounded least happy about.]
>
> Is that fair?"

**Let him answer. Then, and only then:**

> "Some of that is actually what I work on. Do you want me to tell you how I'd think about it, or would
> you rather I stay out of your business and keep asking questions?"

🔴 **THEN STOP TALKING.** That question is the highest-leverage sentence on the call: **it asks
permission to pitch**, so Abdur cannot be accused of pitching something he was invited to say. The
self-deprecating exit at the end is what makes it land. Almost nobody takes it.

⛔ **COACH HARD HERE: do not describe the product inside the reframe.** The reframe plays back and asks
permission. That is all it does. Describing the solution here turns it into a pitch with a reframe's
label on it, and Eddie will see it immediately.

### 5 · SOFT PITCH — **trigger: he said yes to "do you want me to tell you?"** · short, ONE thing

> "The [thing he named]. Something handles that first pass — answers it, gets what's needed, puts it
> where it should go, books the slot. It only ever takes the repetitive end.
>
> I'm not trying to replace anybody. I think that's the mistake too. It's the first touch, then a person
> does the actual work."

🔴 **"I'm not trying to replace anybody, I think that's the mistake too" is MANDATORY.** It is his own
public position handed back as agreement, and it dissolves his only real objection before he raises it.

**Rules while pitching:**
- **One problem.** The one with the biggest number against it.
- **Every sentence tied to something he said.** If he did not raise it, do not solve it.
- ⛔ **NO PRICE.** → *"Depends what we'd actually be fixing. That's what I want to go away and work out."*
- ⛔ **Never "AI receptionist". Never "never miss a call".** Never "we" to imply a team that is not there.
- **Then stop.** 🔴 **The urge to keep talking after a short pitch lands is what kills it.**

### 6 · BOOK THE FOLLOW-UP — **trigger: the pitch landed, or the call is winding down either way**

> "This has been genuinely useful, thank you. Can I do two things?
>
> First, I'll go away and actually act on what you've told me, and I'll come back and tell you what
> happened.
>
> Second, let me map out properly where I think the time is going and what I'd do about it, and walk you
> through it on a proper call. **If you like it, great. If you don't, take the map and use it yourself.
> You won't owe me anything.**
>
> Would later this week or early next work better for you?"

- ⭐ **"I'll come back and tell you what happened"** — a promise, not a request. Costs him nothing and
  is the strongest close available with a mentor-type.
- ⭐ **"Take the map anyway"** removes the trap entirely. Impossible to be defensive against.
- **Two options, never "whenever suits you."** Send the invite while still on the call.
- ⚠️ **If he will not book, that is NOT a failure.** Advice + an open door is a good outcome. Send the
  follow-up and return with a result.

## 🔴 THE CHECK-IN SENTENCES — the spine of the whole call

**Short sentences that hand the floor back and collect a small agreement before moving on.** They are
what turn a monologue into something he is participating in, and each small yes makes the next step
easier for him to accept because people stay consistent with what they have already agreed to.

⭐ **They also surface objections while they are still small.** An unspoken doubt early becomes
"let me think about it" at the end. A check-in drags it into the open while it is still cheap to handle.

### The four that work, and exactly where each one goes

| Sentence | Where | What it actually does |
|---|---|---|
| **"How does that sound to you?"** | End of the frame | **The first yes.** Agreeing to the shape of the call is agreeing to be asked things. Licenses everything after it. |
| **"Have I got that right?"** / **"Tell me if I've got this wrong"** | Opening the reframe | 🔴 **The strongest of the four.** It invites correction rather than agreement, so he keeps authority. Every correction he makes deepens his ownership of the problem. |
| **"Is that fair?"** | After playing his pain back | Converts his scattered answers into one agreed statement. **The pain is now on the record and he put it there.** |
| **"Do you want me to tell you how I'd think about it, or would you rather I keep asking questions?"** | The bridge into any pitch | **Permission to pitch.** Removes reactance completely — he cannot resent being told something he asked for. |

### 🔴 THE RULE THAT MAKES THEM WORK, AND IT IS THE ONLY HARD ONE

**ASK, THEN SHUT UP. WAIT FOR AN ACTUAL ANSWER.**

A check-in that Abdur talks over is worse than not asking, because it signals the agreement was never
really wanted. ⚠️ **Abdur's instinct under pressure is to fill the gap. Coach the silence every time.**

### Why the "invite correction" version beats the "seek agreement" version

⭐ **"Have I got that right?" outperforms "does that make sense?" and it is not close.**

- Seeking agreement asks him to approve Abdur. **Inviting correction asks him to be the expert**, which
  is the role he already holds and enjoys.
- A man who corrects you has engaged with the substance. A man who says "yeah, makes sense" has agreed
  to nothing and may just be being polite.
- 🔴 **With an ex-COO who audits businesses for a living, being handed the red pen is flattering.
  Being asked to approve is faintly insulting.**

### ⛔ THE VERSIONS THAT WILL LOSE HIM

**Classic tie-downs. He has heard every one of these and he teaches people to use them.**

| Never say | Why |
|---|---|
| "Wouldn't you agree?" | Textbook tie-down. Transparent. |
| "Does that make sense?" | Faintly condescending, implies he might not follow. |
| "Fair enough?" after a claim | Pressure dressed as politeness. |
| "You'd want that, right?" | Answers the question for him. |
| "Can I ask you a question?" | He already agreed to be asked questions. Wastes a turn. |
| Any check-in used twice in a row | ⚠️ **Becomes a verbal tic and reads as technique.** |

🔴 **FOUR OR FIVE IN THE WHOLE CALL. NOT MORE.** The power is in the scarcity. Stacked, they stop being
invitations and start being a close he can see coming, which with this particular prospect is fatal.

### Where they go, in order

```
AFTER the frame, before any question    "How does that sound to you?"
OPENING the playback                    "Tell me if I've got this wrong..."
AFTER the playback                      "Is that fair?"
BEFORE saying anything about the build  "Do you want me to tell you, or...?"
AT THE END                              "Would later this week or early next?"
```

⭐ **Notice the shape: three open invitations, then one closed choice at the very end.** Everything
before the booking leaves him room. Only the booking narrows it.

⚠️ **Fire them on the MOMENT, never on the clock.** If the playback arrives six minutes in because he
talked fast, run it there. If Abdur never reaches a pitch at all, the first, second and fifth still
apply and the call is still a success.

## ⛔ THE SEVEN WAYS THIS CALL IS LOST
