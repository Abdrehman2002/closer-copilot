# Demo video — script for the person playing the prospect

Role: **Dave**, owner of a small HVAC shop. 50s, practical, skeptical, money-driven, not a
tech person. Not hostile — interested, but pushing back the way a real owner does.

Everything below is grounded in `products/vextria-hvac.md`, so every card the coach fires can
be fact-checked against real pricing and real claims. Going off-script is fine; going off-script
about **numbers** is what makes the coach withhold a line and the demo look broken. See
"Don't ask these" at the bottom.

---

## The one rule that makes or breaks the recording

**Finish your sentence, then stop talking and stay quiet for a beat.**

The coach fires when it hears you stop. If you trail off, think out loud, or run two thoughts
together, it either fires early or waits. One clean sentence → pause → the card lands in about
a second. That pause is the product; don't fill it.

Same for the closer: let the card appear before you start talking. On camera it reads as
composure, and it's the whole point of the video.

---

## Before you hit record

- Set the meeting goal to **one-call close** (that's what drives the coach to the ask).
- Product: **Vextria HVAC**.
- Create the client as a **new** deal, so Client Brain starts clean.
- Do one throwaway warm-up call first — the very first request of a session is slower
  (measured ~2.5s vs ~1.2s after). Don't record the cold one.

## The script

### 0 · Context — gives the coach something to work with (~20s)

> **Dave:** "Yeah, we're a small shop. Me and two techs, mostly AC replacement around the valley."

*(pause)*

> **Dave:** "Probably forty, forty-five calls a week. More in the summer."

*(pause)*

> **Dave:** "Average job is about nine grand, give or take."

*(pause — these two numbers matter more than anything else you say)*

**Why:** these two figures unlock the strongest moment in the demo. The server does the
arithmetic the moment you say them and hands the coach the result, so the card comes back with
the loss already calculated:

> *"Forty-five calls a week — at nine grand a job, even two missed calls closing means
> **eighteen thousand** walking out."*

Skip either number and the coach can only speak in generalities. Give both and it does the math
on camera. Say them as separate sentences with a pause between.

---

### 1 · "I've already got that covered"

> **Dave:** "Honestly, we already have an answering service. So I don't really see why I'd pay more."

*(pause)*

---

### 2 · The trust objection

> **Dave:** "My customers are mostly older folks. They're gonna hate talking to a robot."

*(pause)*

---

### 3 · The one that's actually about fear

> **Dave:** "And what happens when it quotes somebody wrong and I lose that job? That's my name on the truck, not yours."

*(pause)*

**Why:** this is the best objection in the script. It sounds like a product question but it's
fear, and the coach should isolate that rather than defend the software. Good beat to show.

---

### 4 · Price

> **Dave:** "Alright. So how much is this even gonna run me?"

*(pause — the closer gives $4,000 setup + $797/mo; "four thousand one time" is fine to say now)*

> **Dave:** "Four grand? That's a lot right now."

*(pause)*

**Why:** watch the card here. The sentence streams onto the screen but the **number stays
hidden** until it's been checked against the product file. That's a feature worth pointing at
in the voiceover — the coach cannot invent a price.

---

### 5 · The cheaper competitor

> **Dave:** "My buddy said he saw something like this for around three hundred a month. Why is yours so much more?"

*(pause)*

---

### 6 · Burned before

> **Dave:** "I've been burned before. Paid an agency six months and got nothing out of it."

*(pause)*

---

### 7 · The stall

> **Dave:** "I don't know, man. Let me think about it. Maybe run it by my brother."

*(pause)*

**Why:** shows the coach isolating instead of accepting the stall — "is it the price, or whether
it'll actually work for your shop?" This is the objection every closer loses to, so it lands.

---

### 8 · Turn — the buying signal

> **Dave:** "Okay. That's not bad. What would we actually need to do to get started?"

*(pause)*

> **Dave:** "And how fast could you get it running?"

*(pause — closer closes: live in days, month-to-month, 30-day money-back)*

---

## Short cut for a 60–90 second version

If the video needs to be tight, use only these four and you still show the full range —
objection, price, competitor, close:

**Beat 1** (answering service) → **Beat 4** (price) → **Beat 5** ($300 competitor) → **Beat 8** (get started).

---

## Don't ask these — they'll make the coach go quiet

Not bugs. The coach is built to say nothing rather than make something up, which is correct on
a real call and looks like a bug on video.

| Don't ask | Why |
|---|---|
| "Who else uses this? Any customers I can call?" | There are no clients yet. The honest answer is no testimonials, so it will hold or deflect. |
| "What's your uptime? What accuracy do you guarantee?" | No sourced figure exists. The number would be withheld. |
| "Can you do it for two grand?" | Floor is $3,500, and only in trade. It won't go there, and the moment stalls. |
| Two questions in one breath | The coach answers the last thing it heard. Ask one, pause, then ask the next. |

---

## Worth capturing on screen besides the live cards

- The **delivery marks** on a card — the pauses, the ↗ ↘ pitch cues. Nobody else does this;
  it's the difference between a suggestion and something you can actually say out loud.
- **Client Brain** after the call — run a short second call with the same client and show it
  remembering the objection from the first one. That's the part that isn't a wrapper.
- The **post-call review** and delivery stats (talk ratio, longest monologue).
