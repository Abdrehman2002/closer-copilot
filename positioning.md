# Closer Copilot — USPs and competitive positioning

For whoever is selling this. Framed the way we actually sell: not "here is a feature", but
**"here is what it costs you not to have it."** A closer doesn't buy capability, they buy the
deal they stopped losing.

Competitor facts come from `AIOS/references/closer-copilot-competitor-analysis.md` (researched
2026-07-26 against live competitor sites). Read the last section before you claim anything not
in here.

## The USPs — each one as a loss

### 1. The bot in your participant list is costing you the truth
Nothing joins the call. Audio comes off your own microphone and your own browser tab. No
participant, no "Notetaker has joined", no consent prompt in front of your buyer.

**What it costs you without this:** the second a prospect clocks a recording bot, they get
careful. They stop telling you the real reason the last vendor failed, the real budget, the real
politics. You never find out you lost — you just get a polite "send me a packet." Every guarded
call is a discovery you paid for and didn't get.

> "It runs on your side of the call. Your prospect never knows it's there — so you get the
> version of the truth people only say when nobody's recording."

### 2. The right words said flat is still a lost deal
Every card carries delivery notation, positioned at the exact word:

> `I get that —` ⏸ `[softer] most owners said the same…` ↘ `until they counted the` ***missed*** `calls.` ⏸ `↗ what's one job worth to you?`

Pauses, falling pitch on certainty, rising on genuine curiosity, the one word to stress, when to
lean in.

**What it costs you without this:** you deliver a perfect rebuttal in a flat, rushed, uncertain
voice and it dies. On a voice call, tonality *is* the sale. A statement said like a question gets
treated like a question. Abstrakt gives you the words. Cluely gives you the words. Neither tells
you how to land them — so you get half the job and the half you can already do yourself.

> "Any AI writes you a sentence. This tells you where to pause and which word to hit. That's the
> part that actually closes."

### 3. One invented number and the deal is gone permanently
Every figure the coach wants you to say is checked against your product knowledge, this deal's
history, and what was actually said on this call. If it can't be sourced, it rewrites once — and
if it still can't, it says nothing.

**This is not theoretical.** Under pressure testing the model tried to feed the line *"the last
client saw an extra ten to fifteen thousand a month"* — describing a client that does not exist.
It was blocked before it reached the screen.

**What it costs you without this:** you read out a hallucinated price, a fake case study, an
invented guarantee. You can't un-say a number to a buyer. Best case you eat it. Worst case they
check, and you're the vendor who makes things up — which ends that deal and every referral behind
it. Generative tools with no grounding layer will produce a confident fabrication the first time
you're under pressure, which is exactly when you'll trust it.

> "It would rather say nothing than tell you something that isn't true. On a live call with a real
> buyer, that's the only acceptable default."

### 4. Every number they give you that you don't turn into their loss is a close you left behind
When the prospect states a figure, the arithmetic runs **in code** — not in a language model,
which gets it wrong — and comes back as a line you just read:

> "Forty-five calls a week, that's about a hundred and eighty a month. Even two of those closing
> at nine grand is eighteen thousand walking out."

If they tell you their own missed-call number, it uses **theirs** and never argues with them using
an industry average.

**What it costs you without this:** loss aversion is the strongest lever in the room and it only
works in *their* numbers. Nobody does that maths live while listening. So the moment passes, you
stay in generalities, and the pain never becomes real enough to spend money on.

### 5. Re-asking what they already told you burns the trust you spent a call earning
Client Brain carries objections, commitments, stakeholders and stated pain from call to call. Call
two opens knowing what they pushed back on in call one.

**What it costs you without this:** you ask about their timeline again. They notice. You've just
told them this deal isn't important enough for you to remember — and in a multi-call deal, that's
where momentum quietly dies. Abstrakt logs notes into a CRM after the fact. That's a record, not a
memory, and it isn't in the room with you next time.

### 6. Speed: a card that lands after you've already answered is worth nothing
About **one second** from them finishing to words on your screen, ~1.2–1.4s for the complete line.
The sentence streams as it's written, so you're reading before it's finished.

**What it costs you without this:** a three-second gap is dead air the prospect fills, or you fill
badly. Coaching that arrives after the moment isn't coaching, it's a transcript.

### 7. It never guesses who said what
Your microphone and their audio are captured as **two separate channels**. Speaker separation is
structural, not inferred.

**What it costs you without this:** tools that join a meeting get one mixed stream and have to
*guess* who spoke — that's what diarisation is. When it guesses wrong, the coach thinks the
prospect raised an objection you raised, and the card is nonsense. You can't build reliable
coaching on a guessed speaker label. We removed the guess.

## What Abstrakt cannot do

*(abstrakt.ai — the closest real-time competitor, and a genuinely good product for the job it's
built for: contact centres.)*

| | Abstrakt | Closer Copilot |
|---|---|---|
| To get one suggestion | a formal integration built for your phone system (HubSpot, SalesLoft, Five9, Genesys, RingCX), configured during onboarding | share your screen |
| Where it works | the stack it was wired into | video call, dialer, WhatsApp — anywhere audio reaches your browser |
| What it can say | scripts a manager approved in advance | reasoned in the moment, then fact-checked before it reaches you |
| Delivery | the words | the words *and* how to say them |
| Across calls | logs notes into a CRM | carries a running memory into the next conversation |
| Sold to | the manager | the person on the call |

**The loss-aversion line:**
> "Abstrakt is built to make fifty reps on a floor say the same approved thing. It can't say
> anything a manager didn't write down first — so the objection nobody anticipated is the one
> you're on your own for. And that's the one that loses deals."

**Be fair about where they're ahead** — dialer integrations, compliance tracking, manager
dashboards. Say it first. It reads as knowing your market, and a serious buyer finds it in ten
minutes anyway. Then: "all of that serves a call-centre floor. If that's what you're running, buy
theirs."

## What Cluely cannot do

⚠️ **Lower confidence than the Abstrakt section** — Cluely isn't in the sourced research file, so
treat this as category positioning and don't quote specifics at a prospect.

Cluely is a general-purpose real-time overlay — it reads your screen and helps with whatever is on
it. Horizontal by design.

- **It doesn't know your offer.** No product knowledge, no pricing, no playbook — so it can give
  you a plausible sentence, never *your* sentence, and it has nothing to check a number against.
- **It has no grounding layer.** Nothing stops it stating a price or a result that doesn't exist.
  See USP 3 — that's the one that can't be walked back.
- **It has no deal memory.** Call two starts from zero.
- **It gives you words, not delivery.**

**The loss-aversion line:**
> "It's a strong general assistant. But it doesn't know what you charge, what you promise, or what
> this buyer objected to last Tuesday — so the one moment you actually needed it, it's guessing.
> Generic help is fine when you're winging an interview. It isn't enough when someone's deciding
> whether to hand you money."

## The rest of the field, in one line each

- **Gong** — scores the call after it's over. *"Analyses what you did wrong yesterday. We help you win it today."*
- **Nooks / Attention / Balto** — same shape as Abstrakt: per-seat team tools, sold to managers.
- **CloserCoach.ai** — roleplay and post-call scoring, not live execution. No cross-call memory.
- **Hyperbound Perform** — manager tooling on top of call recorders, for rescuing deals from a dashboard, not for the person mid-sentence.
- **Cirrus Insight** — pre-call briefing, and it requires Salesforce.
- **Hebbrix** — context loading, confirmed async, not live.

**The pattern worth naming out loud:** almost everything in this category is sold to a *manager*
watching, or a *coach* reviewing. Ours is the only one built for the person actually talking.

## What we do NOT claim — read before you improvise

The product refuses to state facts it can't source. Hold yourself to the same standard. A rep
repeating a stale competitor claim is the same failure — with the added problem that a buyer can
check it in one search.

- **No customer results, no case studies, no named clients.** There aren't any yet. Do not invent
  one, do not imply one.
- **No uptime or accuracy guarantees.** No sourced figure exists.
- **Don't quote competitor pricing or feature lists** beyond what's in the research file, and
  never for Cluely. If pressed on specifics, tell them to check the competitor's own site rather
  than take your word — that answer builds more trust than a confident guess.
- **We are not** a dialer, a CRM, a compliance system, or a manager dashboard.

**When you can't prove a claim, run the demo.** A card appearing a second after a real objection,
with the delivery marks on it, does more than any slide — and it's the one piece of proof that
needs no citation.
