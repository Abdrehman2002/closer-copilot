## The five USPs, in the order that wins deals

### 1. Nothing joins the call
Audio comes off your own microphone and your browser tab. There is no bot, no participant, no
"Notetaker has joined the meeting", no calendar invite to a third party, no recording consent
prompt appearing in front of your buyer.

**Why a closer cares:** the moment a prospect sees a note-taking bot in the participant list,
the call changes. They get guarded, they stop telling you the messy truth, and some of them ask
you to remove it. Your coaching should be invisible — it's yours, not theirs.

**How to say it:** "It runs on your side of the call. Your prospect never knows it exists."

### 2. It tells you HOW to say it, not just what
Every card carries delivery notation, positioned at the exact word:

> `I get that —` ⏸ `[softer] most owners said the same…` ↘ `until they counted the` ***missed*** `calls.` ⏸ `↗ what's one job worth to you?`

Pauses, falling pitch on certainty, rising on genuine curiosity, the one word to stress, when to
lean in. Not a mood label at the top of the card — marks sitting where you need them.

**Why a closer cares:** the right line delivered flat loses the deal. Tonality *is* the sale on a
voice call — certainty on the price, curiosity on the question, silence after the ask. Anything
that gives you words without delivery is giving you half the job.

**How to say it:** "Any AI can write you a sentence. This one tells you where to pause and which
word to hit — that's the part that actually closes."

### 3. It cannot make things up
Every number the coach wants you to say is checked against your own product knowledge, this
deal's history, and what was actually said on the call. If it can't source it, it rewrites once,
and if it still can't, it stays silent rather than feed you a fabrication.

**This is not theoretical.** Under pressure testing, the model tried to say *"the last client saw
an extra ten to fifteen thousand a month"* — about a client that does not exist. It was blocked
before it reached the screen.

**Why a closer cares:** you cannot un-say a number to a buyer. A hallucinated price, a made-up
case study, an invented guarantee — that's not a bug you apologise for, it's the deal and
sometimes the reputation. Every other AI in this space will happily generate a confident number.

**How to say it:** "It would rather say nothing than tell you something that isn't true. On a live
call with a real buyer, that's the only acceptable default."

### 4. It does the arithmetic for you, live
When the prospect gives you a number, the maths is computed in code — not by a language model,
which gets it wrong — and handed to you as a line you can read:

> "Forty-five calls a week, that's about a hundred and eighty a month. Even two of those closing
> at nine grand is eighteen thousand walking out."

If they tell you their own missed-call number, it uses theirs and never argues with them using an
industry average.

**Why a closer cares:** loss aversion is the strongest lever in the room, and it only works with
*their* numbers. Doing that maths in your head, live, while listening — nobody does it reliably.

**How to say it:** "The second they give you a number, you get the cost of their problem back in
their own figures, already worked out."

### 5. It remembers the deal, not just the call
Client Brain carries objections, commitments, stakeholders and stated pain from call to call. The
second conversation opens knowing what they pushed back on in the first.

**Why a closer cares:** deals are won across calls. Re-asking something they already told you is
the fastest way to look like you weren't listening.

## "Why not Cluely?"

Cluely is a general-purpose assistant that reads your screen and helps with whatever is on it.
That's a different product for a different job.

**The distinction to draw:** it is horizontal, we are a closer's tool.

- It doesn't know your offer, your pricing, or your playbook — so it can give you a plausible
  sentence, but not *your* sentence, and it has nothing to fact-check a number against.
- It has no memory of this deal. Call two starts from nothing.
- It gives you words, not delivery.

**The line:** "It's a smart general assistant. This is built for one job — closing — and it knows
your offer, your prices and this specific buyer. Generic help is fine for an interview. It isn't
enough when someone's about to hand you money."

## "Why not Abstrakt?"

Abstrakt sits in the contact-centre category — real-time guidance built for large teams working
from a script, with compliance and manager oversight at scale.

**The distinction to draw:** script adherence for a floor of reps vs. coaching one closer through
a consultative deal.

- That category optimises for consistency across many reps on repeatable calls. A closer running
  discovery and multi-call deals needs judgement, memory and delivery, not script compliance.
- It's a heavier deployment — a platform decision, usually with implementation.

**The line:** "That's built to make fifty reps say the same thing on a script. This is built to make
one closer better at a deal that takes three conversations. If you're running a call centre, they're
a reasonable fit. If you're closing, they're solving a different problem."

## What we do NOT claim — read this before you improvise

The product refuses to state facts it can't source. Hold yourself to the same standard: a rep
repeating a stale competitor claim is the same failure, and it's the one thing a buyer will
actually check.

- **Don't quote competitor pricing, feature lists or integrations from this document.** Nothing
  here about Cluely or Abstrakt is verified — it's category positioning, not fact. If a prospect
  asks specifics, say you'd rather they check the competitor's own site than take your word.
- **No customer results, no case studies, no named clients.** There aren't any yet. Do not
  invent one. The demo is the proof — run it live.
- **No uptime or accuracy guarantees.** No sourced figure exists.
- **We are not** a dialer, a CRM, a compliance system, or a manager dashboard.

**When you have nothing to prove a claim with, run the demo instead.** Watching a card appear a
second after a real objection, with the delivery marks on it, does more than any slide.
